## Part 1 — Plain English, for the owner

**What you asked for, and what you will get.**

1. *"Book a slot in the calendar with the same single-use link as the one through the email."* Yes, with a plain limit. **In this release the only calendar we can write into is Cal.com.** A clinic on Acuity, Mindbody, Boulevard, Zenoti, Vagaro, GoHighLevel or PatientNow keeps today's typed confirmation until that platform gains a write adapter (the connector port is read-only today, `packages/connectors/src/port.ts:30-89`; `git grep -il calcom` returns nothing, so Cal.com is a new integration). For a Cal.com clinic the desk's `/booking/<token>` page shows real free slots for the next 14 days, highlights the days the person named, and **"Confirm this time" both records the appointment in healdesk and writes it into the clinic's calendar**. If the calendar write fails, the appointment is still recorded and the page tells the desk to add it by hand. Three things must be true before any live clinic gets a calendar write: the clinic has signed the BAA revision that says what we write to its calendar (today's signed BAA says we hold no credentials and read nothing — a Cal.com connection would make that sentence false); Cal.com has signed our BAA **or** the clinic uses its own Cal.com account under its own agreement, and we have recorded which in the console; and Cal.com's own confirmation emails and workflows are off on the event type, because we give Cal.com a healdesk-owned throwaway address, never the desk's inbox and never a patient's.

2. *"A fourth option: decline + reschedule."* Yes. It is called **"Can't do that — propose other times"**. The desk picks up to three alternatives (from the calendar when connected, typed otherwise). How the person hears them depends on the channel, because the law does: on the phone we may say times; on WhatsApp, Instagram or Messenger we may not. So: if the person agreed, on their original call or thread, to an automated call-back, our assistant rings them, offers the times and books the one they choose; if they did not agree, the desk rings them (a human dialling is outside the robocall rules); a Meta thread only ever gets a fixed line saying the practice has alternatives and will call. Alternatives lapse after 72 hours, or when the first proposed slot passes, or when the request itself expires, whichever is first.

3. *"Confirm a time should accept the calendar spot."* Yes — that is exactly what the slot picker does. When the desk confirms a time for a person who agreed to a call-back — on the phone or on Meta — the assistant rings them to say so; otherwise the page tells the desk to call. The cases that still need a human call: no consent recorded, the clinic's callbacks switched off, Sunday or outside 8am–8pm, or two attempts already failed.

4. *"Every agent asks for a primary time and two secondaries."* Yes, on the phone and on Meta. The desk page and the emails show all three; the callback agent reads them back. The website demo asks the same way so prospects see the behaviour, but there is no web booking channel in the product: web requests do not exist until one is built.

5. *"Some clinics want the bot to fully book directly."* Yes, **on the phone only**, and only when six things are true: the clinic has connected Cal.com (the only writable calendar) with its BAA basis recorded; the clinic has accepted a standalone **Direct booking addendum** (it takes responsibility for what the assistant books, accepted by a named person on the same one-page setup link described in item 7); counsel has approved that addendum's text — until then the section does not appear on the page and the switch cannot be turned; the clinic is live with an approved script; you have switched the clinic's mode to "direct" in the console; and a platform-wide kill switch is on. The desk still gets an email with the link, but as an FYI, not a decision. Meta channels never book: Meta signs no BAA and its terms ban health data, and "you're booked Tuesday at 2" on WhatsApp is health data.

6. *"Never text. Send a WhatsApp if the client bought Meta, and an outbound call that ingests what was sent to the clinic and fills in the minimum information."* Mostly yes, with legal corrections:
   - **The outbound call is only legal if the person agreed to it first.** An AI voice is an "artificial voice" under the TCPA, so "they gave us their number" is not consent. Both the phone agent and the Meta bot ask one question — on the phone: *"Is it alright if the practice's automated assistant calls you back on this number about this booking request — to settle the time, or if the front desk still needs a detail from you? It's an AI voice, and you can say 'stop' at any time and we won't call again."* — and we keep the person's exact words in reply (on Meta, the raw message they typed, not a summary). No recorded consent, no dial: the call table refuses a row whose consent does not match that clinic and that number. The callback is placed only 8am–8pm **in the practice's time zone, which we treat as the person's** (we hold no better time zone for them; you confirm each clinic's zone in the console before callbacks can be switched on), never on a Sunday, at most twice per request and at most four times to one number in thirty days, at least four hours apart, never after the request expires, and never again when the first attempt's outcome is unknown. It opens by saying it is an AI, that it is transcribed, the practice's phone number, and how to stop it; "stop" or pressing 1 bars that number during the call, before anything else happens.
   - **The callback "ingests" only what the request already holds** (first name, the times they gave, which required fields are still missing) and collects only fields from a closed list you approve at onboarding. Nothing clinical can be on that list, nothing about a condition is ever spoken by us, and we ask for nothing we cannot use (no email address, no "how did you hear about us").
   - **WhatsApp is only possible when the person came to you on WhatsApp.** Inside 24 hours of their last message we may send a fixed, PHI-free line (already built). Outside 24 hours Meta requires a pre-approved template and the person's opt-in; the bot asks for that opt-in as its own question, separately from the call-back question, and we submit one utility template naming no time, service or provider. Whether that template may be sent at all waits on a counsel answer, so it sits behind its own switch. A phone caller has no WhatsApp thread; we will not ask them for a WhatsApp number.

7. *"Establish the minimum information during onboarding, automatically, immediately after purchase."* Yes. The moment a purchase provisions, the buyer is emailed one single-use link, `/setup/<token>` (14 days, same mechanism as the connect link), opening a one-page form: the fields the assistant will collect for a booking request, pre-selected from a closed catalogue by specialty, with the fields that can never be asked (date of birth, insurance, anything medical) listed as excluded. The same form carries the optional direct-booking election once counsel has approved its text. Submitting stamps a new onboarding step. **If nobody answers within 14 days the specialty defaults apply automatically**; you can change them from the console at any time. No account, no login.

**What cannot be done as stated, and the lawful version.**

| Ask | Why not as stated | Lawful version |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The bot books on Meta | Meta signs no BAA; Business Tools Terms §1.h ban health data; a booked time on a Meta thread identifies a patient of a named provider | Meta stays a front door; direct booking is voice-only |
| Call people back because they left a number | FCC 24-17: AI voice = artificial voice; §227(b) needs prior express consent | Capture consent verbatim on the inbound contact; dial only with a matching consent row; otherwise the desk calls |
| Reminders / win-backs by callback | Promotional needs prior express *written* consent; §227(c) DNC exposure; healthcare exemption unavailable while covered-entity status is unsettled | Stays off; only three transactional callback purposes exist |
| WhatsApp a phone caller | No thread → business-initiated → template + opt-in + a number we would have to ask for | Do not ask; WhatsApp only for WhatsApp threads |
| Put a time in the WhatsApp update | PHI on a no-BAA vendor | Fixed PHI-free line / PHI-free template; times only by phone |
| One "single-use" link doing several things | The link is single-*decision* by trigger (0039) | Extend the trigger so "propose other times" is a non-final step and the same link is later confirmed once |
| Calls "in the person's own time" | We do not know it; every contact carries the clinic's zone, and every clinic provisioned through checkout is `America/New_York` unless someone sets it | Say what is true — the practice's zone — and refuse to dial until an operator has confirmed the clinic's zone |

**What changes for you operationally.** Console actions (nine): resend setup link, edit requirements, set booking mode, switch callbacks on, confirm the clinic's time zone, record a Cal.com connection's BAA basis, set the WhatsApp template name, retry a calendar write, view failed calendar writes. Per clinic, by hand until the runtime-context action is verified with Synthflow: paste the rendered required-fields line and extractor list into the inbound agent on every change to the field set or the booking mode (the console switch alone does not change what the phone says); create the clinic's callback agent by cloning the platform template (recording storage off, verified by a test call); one Cal.com event type with emails and workflows off; one Meta template submission per WhatsApp clinic. Platform-wide, once: a Synthflow workspace on the US cluster (the existing workspace is on the EU "Global" cluster, `docs/blockers-runbook.md:50-59`), a discard mailbox for the Cal.com attendee address, the 15-day subcontractor notice to every signed clinic before the first Cal.com write, and the counsel questions in I.6 before any callback is placed.

**What this design deliberately does not do:** no text messages, no reminders or reactivation, no Meta thread carrying a time/provider/service/condition, no clinic login, no third notification channel, no patient email.
