  electronically in connection with a transaction covered by HIPAA and is
  therefore a covered entity.

  Practice's status is Practice's determination, not healdesk's. Whatever it
  is, healdesk applies the safeguards in this agreement. Where Practice is a
  covered entity, this agreement is a business associate agreement under 45
  CFR 164.502(e) and 164.504(e). Where it is not, this agreement is a
  data-protection agreement on the same terms, with the additions in the
  clause headed "Where Practice is not a covered entity". Practice will tell
  healdesk promptly if its status changes.

  Whatever Practice's status, state breach-notification and
  medical-confidentiality laws may also apply to both parties. Where Practice
  is not a covered entity, state consumer-privacy and consumer-health-privacy
  laws may apply as well. Some of these laws carry a private right of action.

3. WHAT HEALDESK MAY DO WITH PATIENT INFORMATION

  healdesk may use and disclose PHI only as necessary to provide the Services

  statistics" permits, and as required by law. Except for what that clause
  permits, healdesk may not use or disclose PHI in a way that would violate

  information for Practice under Cal. Civ. Code 56.10(c)(3), whether or not
  Practice is a covered entity. healdesk will not further disclose that

  following; 11 CCR 7000 and following), the Washington My Health My Data Act

  the extent each applies (together, "the state privacy laws"). Where this

6. GOOGLE CALENDAR

  and every term of this clause applies to each of them.

  Practice has told healdesk that this account belongs to Practice's Google
  Workspace organisation and that its administrator has accepted Google's
  business associate agreement. Practice will record a calendar election
  promptly if either stops being true. healdesk cannot see whether Google's
  agreement was accepted, and relies on Practice's statement. When Practice
  chooses a calendar, and again before adding each appointment, healdesk
  checks that the connected account belongs to a Google Workspace or Cloud
  Identity organisation and that the calendar belongs to that same
  organisation. It adds nothing to a calendar that fails either check.

  healdesk reads the names of the calendars on which that account has owner
  access, so that Practice can choose; when the calendar is free or busy; and
  the appointments healdesk itself added to it. Google's permissions also

  data after the Services end.

  healdesk adds an appointment to the calendar when the assistant books one,

  appointment carries a first name, the service and the time, and no other
  information about the patient or the person booking. It never carries a
  telephone number, an email address, or anything clinical beyond the name of
  the service. The first name is the one given by the person booking, who may
  not be the patient; where that name cannot be used, it is the word
  "Patient". The service is named as in Practice's own service list; where
  none was given or its name cannot be used, the appointment says
  "Appointment". healdesk marks each appointment private, invites no one to it
  and sets no reminders on it. It moves, edits or cancels nothing it did not
  create. Google keeps a deleted event in the calendar's trash for up to 30
  days under Google's own terms.

  healdesk may de-identify Practice's PHI in accordance with 45 CFR

  healdesk keeps PHI at rest only in the United States, always encrypted, and
  lets a subcontractor handle PHI only if that subcontractor does the same. In
  transit, PHI is encrypted wherever healdesk controls both ends, and healdesk
  will not transmit PHI over a channel that cannot negotiate encryption. An
  email whose receiving server will not negotiate encryption is failed, not
  sent, and healdesk tells Practice rather than send it in the clear. The list
  of individuals in a breach report travels by secure transfer, never in the
  email itself.

  One narrow exception: a person who telephones Practice has chosen the
  telephone, and a telephone call is not end-to-end encrypted. healdesk may
  answer such a call, and what the assistant says on it is limited to the
  minimum necessary and never includes clinical detail, test results or card
  numbers. Whenever the assistant is not answering, including for as long
  after the Services end as the Terms provide, healdesk may pass such a call
  through, unchanged, to the direct line Practice gives it. Where Practice
  switches on live call transfer, the assistant may also transfer a caller to
  the number Practice gives for that purpose. What is said after that is
  between the caller and Practice. healdesk places no calls to patients and
  sends no SMS, except that the assistant is engineered and designed to offer
  a caller a single text message that carries fixed wording and Practice's own
  payment link, where Practice has chosen that option. This exception, that
  text included, does not extend to any other channel or content.

11. MINIMUM NECESSARY

  Each party will limit its use, disclosure and requests of PHI to the minimum
  necessary to accomplish the purpose, consistent with 45 CFR 164.502(b).

  When someone wants to book, the assistant asks the person booking for no
  more than a receptionist would write on a callback slip:

    a. on every channel: a first name, a telephone number Practice can call, a
       preferred day or time, and one or two other times that could work;
    b. on a call or in a website chat, where Practice's settings or its
       specialty's defaults ask for them: a last name and the service asked
       about; and
    c. on a call or in a website chat, on the same condition: whether the
       person is a new or returning patient, whether the visit is a
       consultation or a treatment, and any practitioner they would like to
       see.

  It asks for no email address and no history, medication or diagnosis, and it
  never takes a card number. Anything else a person chooses to say or write on
  a call or in a website chat is kept only in the transcript of that
  conversation, for no longer than the next paragraph allows. healdesk does
  not keep the messages a person sends on Instagram, Messenger or WhatsApp.
  From those channels it keeps only the details in (a) (a first name and a
  telephone number, each checked by automated filters designed to keep
  clinical content out, and preferred times only in a fixed day-and-time form)
  and the identifier Meta attaches to the conversation. On every channel the
  assistant's opening line says that it is an AI assistant and, on a call,
  that the call is transcribed; Practice cannot switch that statement off or
  edit it out.

  healdesk does not keep transcripts. When a call or a website chat ends,
  healdesk emails its transcript to the email address on Practice's healdesk
  account, and to no other address, only over an encrypted connection, as the
  Safeguards clause provides. So that an email that fails or is returned can
  be sent again, healdesk holds one encrypted copy of the transcript only
  until Practice's mail server confirms that it has received the email, and
  then deletes the copy at once. If a send fails or the email is returned,
  healdesk keeps the copy and sends the email again until it is delivered. If
  the email is accepted for delivery but neither its delivery nor its failure
  is confirmed within 72 hours of its last sending, healdesk deletes the copy
  then. In no case does healdesk hold the copy for more than 30 days after the
  transcript was queued for sending: a copy whose email has not been delivered
  by then is deleted. While it holds the copy, healdesk may also send the
  email again when Practice asks. healdesk keeps a record of each transcript's
  delivery, without what was said or written in it, for as long as it keeps
  the record of that conversation. It keeps no other copy of a transcript,
  apart from the backups described below; what Anthropic keeps is what the
  Subcontractors clause describes. healdesk's voice provider holds the
  transcript and recording of a call only while the call runs and until
  healdesk has taken its own copy of the transcript, and healdesk then has the
  provider delete them; the provider's own retention for anything left is one
  day. Once healdesk's copy is deleted, the email is Practice's own record of
  what was said, and Practice is responsible for keeping, protecting and
  deleting it.

  healdesk keeps the record of each conversation, without what was said or
  written in it, for 90 days after it took place, and each appointment record
  for 90 days after the appointment's time. It keeps each booking request for
  90 days after the latest of when it was made, the time it was confirmed for
  and its appointment's time. A person's contact details are kept while any
  conversation, appointment record or booking request of theirs is kept, and
  in any case until 90 days after the last time those details were recorded or
  changed. A backup copy of anything healdesk deletes expires within 35 days
  of the deletion. healdesk keeps no call audio; its voice provider holds a
  call's recording only as this clause describes. The Term clause governs what
  is held after termination.

12. REPORTING BREACHES AND SECURITY INCIDENTS

  healdesk will notify Practice, by email to Practice's address in the Notices
  clause, of any of the following without unreasonable delay and in no case
  later than 60 calendar days after discovery:

    a. any use or disclosure of PHI that this agreement does not permit, by
       healdesk or by a subcontractor;
    b. any breach of unsecured PHI;
    c. any security incident, including anything a state breach-notification
       law treats as a breach of the security of PHI.

  Routine unsuccessful attempts that compromised nothing, such as blocked
  sign-ins, port scans and filtered traffic, are security incidents that the
  parties agree this clause reports once, here; healdesk will summarise them
  on request.

  A breach is treated as discovered on the first day healdesk knows of it, or
  by exercising reasonable diligence would have known of it, as 45 CFR
  164.410(a)(2) provides. healdesk knows what any of its workforce members or
  agents, other than the person committing the breach, knows or by exercising
  reasonable diligence would have known. Anything else this clause covers is
  discovered when healdesk becomes aware of it.

  healdesk delays a notice only where a law enforcement official states that
  the notice would impede a criminal investigation or damage national
  security. It then delays it only for as long as 45 CFR 164.412, or the state
  law that applies, provides, and gives it as soon as that delay ends.

  Each notice is also a written report, sent to the same address and as
  complete as healdesk can then make it, and healdesk will add to the report
  promptly as information becomes available. For a breach, the report will
  include, to the extent possible, what 45 CFR 164.410(c) requires and what
  Practice needs for its own notices:

  · the identification of each individual whose unsecured PHI has been, or is
    reasonably believed by healdesk to have been, accessed, acquired, used or
    disclosed during the breach, sent by secure transfer as the Safeguards
    clause provides;
  · a brief description of what happened, including the date of the breach and
    the date of its discovery;
  · the types of unsecured PHI involved;
  · any steps individuals should take to protect themselves from potential
    harm resulting from the breach;
  · what healdesk is doing to investigate the breach, to mitigate harm to
    individuals and to protect against further breaches;
  · whether a law enforcement official required a notice to be delayed;
  · a contact at healdesk; and
  · any other available information Practice must include in a notice that 45
    CFR 164.404(c) or a state breach-notification law requires of it.

  For anything else this clause covers, the report says what happened and
  when, what information was involved, and what healdesk has done about it.
  Who bears the cost of notifying patients, regulators and anyone else about a
  breach is allocated by the Liability clause.

  164.504(e)(5). healdesk remains answerable to Practice for its
  subcontractors' acts and omissions with that PHI.

  Language models are used to understand a patient and answer them during a
  conversation. healdesk's language-model provider is Anthropic. Anthropic
  receives the content of each conversation, to generate the assistant's

  agreement only with that standard retention, which healdesk does not
  control. Two exceptions apply even then: content its automated
  trust-and-safety systems flag as violating its usage policy, which it may
  keep for up to two years, and content the law requires it to keep, which it
  may keep for as long as the law requires. Anthropic does not use that
  content to train its models without healdesk's permission, which healdesk
  will not give. healdesk may not use PHI to train, fine-tune or evaluate any
  model, its own or a third party's.

  healdesk will give Practice its current list of its subcontractors on
  Practice's written request to healdesk's email address in the Notices

14. PATIENT ACCESS, AMENDMENT AND ACCOUNTING

  When a patient asks to see their information, to have it corrected, or to be
  told who it was disclosed to, Practice answers them. healdesk gives Practice
  what it needs to answer within ten business days of being asked:

    a. Access. healdesk will make available the PHI it holds about that
       patient in a designated record set, so Practice can meet its
       obligations under 45 CFR 164.524.
    b. Amendment. healdesk will make that information available for amendment
       and will incorporate any amendment Practice directs, under 45 CFR
       164.526. healdesk corrects a person's contact details in its own copy.
       It does not rewrite a booking request, an appointment record or the
       record of a conversation: it appends the amendment to that record and
       links the two, as 45 CFR 164.526(c)(1) provides. Every later export of
       that record, and every screen of healdesk's that shows it, then carries
       the amendment.
    c. Accounting. healdesk will maintain and make available the information
       Practice needs to give a patient an accounting of disclosures under 45
       CFR 164.528.

  healdesk holds a transcript only to deliver it to Practice, and only for as
  long as the clause headed "Minimum necessary" allows. Once the copy is
  deleted, healdesk has no transcript to make available, amend, export or
  delete: the email that carried it is Practice's own record, and Practice
  answers a request about a transcript from that email.

  If a patient makes one of these requests, or any other request about their
  information, to healdesk itself rather than to Practice, healdesk will not
  act on it. It will pass the request to Practice within five business days
  and, in its reply to the patient, tell them that healdesk cannot act on it
  and has passed it to Practice.

15. WHERE HEALDESK ACTS IN PRACTICE'S PLACE

  Some of what healdesk does is Practice carrying out its own duties through
  healdesk, rather than healdesk doing something of its own. Answering a
  patient who calls, and replying in Practice's name to a patient who writes,
  are both of those. Where healdesk carries out an obligation the HIPAA
  Privacy Rule places on Practice, healdesk will meet the requirements that
  Rule places on Practice in performing it, and not only the lighter ones it
  places on a business associate. 45 CFR 164.504(e)(2)(ii)(H).

16. WHAT HEALDESK IS NOT AUTHORISED TO DO

  · Use PHI to train, fine-tune, or evaluate any artificial intelligence
    model, its own or a third party's.
  · Disclose PHI to any advertising or analytics platform, in any form,
    including hashed or aggregated, except by replying on Instagram, Messenger
    or WhatsApp as the paragraphs below describe.
  · Provide clinical advice, diagnosis, triage, or treatment recommendations
    through the assistant.
  · Sell, license, or otherwise monetise patient information.

  Instagram, Messenger and WhatsApp are operated by Meta, which signs no
  business associate agreement and keeps what passes through those channels. A
  reply there therefore discloses to Meta at least that the person is in
  contact with Practice. Practice connects these channels only if it chooses
  to, knowing this, and may switch them off at any time. healdesk gives Meta
  nothing for advertising or analytics.

  Where Practice connects them, the assistant replies there only inside a
  conversation the person started, on the channel the person chose, and only
  within the window Meta allows. Its first reply in each conversation says
  that it is an AI assistant and that the channel is not secure. healdesk
  checks each message a person sends there for clinical content before the
  language model sees it, and keeps no message there and no summary of one.
  The assistant is instructed not to repeat clinical content, name a treatment
  or confirm appointment details there, and to move anything clinical to a
  call or to Practice's own secure route. Each reply the language model writes
  there is checked by automated filters designed to keep those out, and a
  reply the filters refuse is replaced by a fixed one. No filter catches every
  wording, so Practice should not invite patients to send clinical information
  on these channels.
